= Slidedown Design: Zoom Transition
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

====
Status: implemented. The canvas described under <<canvas>> is not built and
may never need to be.
====

== What this is

Prezi's one good idea was that moving the camera can say something. A deck
laid out on a plane, with detail nested inside overview, made "advance" mean
"go inside this" — and going back meant coming out again, to the place you
left. The zoom was not decoration; it was an assertion about structure.

Prezi also made every other transition swoop, which is how the idea acquired
its reputation and its nickname, "Prezi nausea".

Slidedown is in an unusual position to take the good half. A nexus deck
already states which slides contain which: a nexus is an overview, and its
spokes are the topics it lists. That relation is computed at build time and
sits in the navigation graph. The movement does not have to be authored,
because the containment is already known.

== The rule

Motion is confined to moves it can honestly describe.

Moves::
Entering a spoke from a nexus, and returning from that spoke to the nexus it
was entered from.

Does not move::
Everything else. A plain advance is a step sideways, not a step inward. Two
consecutive slides do not contain one another, and animating that transition
would assert a relationship the deck does not have.

This is the whole discipline, and it is the difference between the feature
and the cliché.

== Opting in

....
.meta{
  transition: zoom
}
....

The default is `none`, so every deck that does not ask keeps the instant
swap it has always had. An unrecognised value resolves to `none` rather than
reaching the runtime — see `transition_resolve()` in
`src/lib/compiler_rendering.py`. The resolved value is emitted as a
`slideTransition` metadata element beside `slideIDprefix`.

== What flies is the text

The entry in the menu and the heading on the slide are the same thing said
twice. So the transition carries one into the other: the chosen line lifts
off the nexus, rises, and becomes the spoke's `<h1>`. Returning, the heading
falls back into the bullet it came from.

Nothing else travels. The two slides cross-fade underneath.

=== Two approaches that did not work

Both are recorded because both look like they should work.

Scaling the slides past one another::
Grow the outgoing slide about the anchor, bring the incoming one in from
small about the same point. The result reads as a PowerPoint zoom: the whole
frame expands, blanks, and contracts. Nothing the viewer was looking at
before the move is present after it, so there is no target — only a change
of scale, and the eye has nothing to follow.

Flying a miniature of the destination::
Closer, and still wrong. At the size of a menu entry a slide is an
illegible grey rectangle, so the promise — see where you are going — is only
half kept: you get the destination's shape, not its content. It also has to
be painted, and painting it means guessing a background colour, which
visibly corrects itself on landing.

What made Prezi legible was that the destination was already on screen,
small and in place, and the camera merely closed the distance. The
continuity is *material*: the same thing, seen from nearer. A line of text
is the one part of a slide that survives being carried — it reads as text at
every size on the way, and there is nothing to repaint, because the real
slide is what is underneath.

== The choreography

....
beat      the chosen entry is marked and held        160ms
flight    the entry is carried onto the heading      300ms
handover  the copy gives way to the real heading     120ms
....

=== The beat

With a clicker the room cannot see which key was pressed, and a deck that
simply starts moving leaves the audience working out after the fact where it
went. So the entry is outlined in its own `currentColor` and held long
enough to be read, before anything travels. It also parks the eye on the
point the movement is about to start from.

Returns skip it: nothing was selected.

=== The flight

A clone of the chosen line is fixed to the screen at the box it currently
occupies, and transformed to the box the heading will occupy — a translate
plus a scale. Both ends of the shared element are concealed for the
duration, so the text is never on screen twice.

The scale is the ratio of the two *heights*, not their widths. The two
phrasings are rarely the same length, but both are a line of type, and it is
the type that has to match on arrival.

=== The handover

An entry and the heading it becomes are usually not word for word the same,
so the last moment is a short cross-fade rather than a swap. Where the two
do read the same, there is nothing to see — and a deck that wants the effect
at its cleanest should phrase them identically, which turns the handover
into a true morph.

== Ordering, and the bug it exists to prevent

Slides are flex children of `.presentation-viewport`. Two of them shown at
once would stack, one above the other.

That constraint produced the subtlest fault in this feature. The heading's
position was measured while the outgoing slide was still in the flow — so
the incoming slide laid out *beneath* it, and its heading measured about
half a page too low. The carry flew to that position, which meant it went
downwards on the way in. Returning was unaffected and looked perfect,
because there the spoke is genuinely on screen when measured.

"One direction is right and the other is not" is the fingerprint of a
measurement made in the wrong conditions, rather than of a sign error.

So the order is fixed, and the same in both directions:

. `slide_detach()` pins the outgoing slide to the box it already occupies,
  taking it out of the flow without moving it. Layout coordinates
  (`offsetLeft`, `offsetWidth`) are used, so the viewport's scale does not
  enter into it.
. `slide_commit()` performs the swap. The incoming slide is now the only one
  in the flow, and lays out exactly where it will stay.
. The outgoing slide is shown again, pinned, for the cross-fade.
. *Now* the heading is measured, and the copy is built and flown.

Because the swap happens at the start, the fallback path — a spoke with no
`<h1>`, or a copy that cannot be built — has to take the scaffolding back
down rather than skip it. `slide_reattach()` does that, and it runs on every
path out.

== Where the boxes come from

The entry's box is the jump anchor itself: `zoom_anchorRect()` reads it from
the `.sd-jump[data-jump="..."]` element the compiler emits. A return needs
the same box, but by then the nexus is hidden and measures as nothing, so it
is measured when the jump is taken and travels on the return stack alongside
the reveal count, in `return_push()`.

A return also carries no address of its own. The spoke names its own entry,
via `spokeFor()`.

One conversion is needed. The copy is fixed to the screen, which puts it
outside the viewport that scales the deck to the window, so the type it
inherits is in the deck's baseline pixels and would render far too large.
`ghost_create()` reads the computed font size and multiplies it by the
viewport scale.

== When it declines

The deck falls through to the instant swap when any of these hold:

* the deck did not opt in;
* the viewer has `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` set;
* the deck is not a nexus deck;
* the move is not a jump or a return;
* the anchor does not exist, is not revealed, or cannot be measured;
* the viewport cannot be measured;
* a return has no recorded box, as after a deep link into a spoke;
* the spoke has no heading to carry the entry onto;
* the flying copy cannot be built.

Reduced motion is also enforced in CSS, so a page served with a stale
`slidedown.js` still lands on the instant swap.

== Presenters outrun animations

A clicker at a lectern produces input faster than any transition. Rather
than queueing or dropping those presses, a new move snaps the running one to
its end state first: `zoom_cancel()` drains the pending finishers, each of
which completes its leg synchronously. Finishing one leg starts the next,
which is then drained in the same loop, so the deck lands exactly where it
would have landed anyway — immediately.

Every leg carries a timer as well as a `transitionend` listener, because an
element hidden mid-flight never fires the event. The selection beat is a
plain wait with no element to listen to, and takes no such allowance.

== Not built: the canvas

The real Prezi arrangement has every slide laid out on one plane, spokes
nested inside their nexus, so the destination is visible — small, legible,
in place — *before* it is chosen. A menu whose entries were the spokes
themselves would say what each topic looks like, not merely what it is
called.

That needs the slides genuinely stacked rather than swapped: `.slide` moved
to `position: absolute` inside a positioned stage emitted by
`compiler_rendering.py`, with the `display: block` the runtime sets inline
preserved exactly, since that inline value overrides the stylesheet's
`display: flex` and quietly restoring the flex centring would move content
on every existing deck.

It is worth doing only if these decks are ever navigated exploratorily. Where
the nexus is a menu passed through on the way somewhere, carrying the chosen
line across says everything the canvas would, for a great deal less.
